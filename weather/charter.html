The Charter

What the instruments said. Dated, sourced, and never quietly fixed.

The five rules

  1. Every number carries its source and its timestamp. No orphan claims.
  2. No fabricated or interpolated data without explicit marking. (Reconstructions are labeled reconstructions.)
  3. Gaps are shown, not hidden. A silent source is displayed as silent.
  4. The record is permanent. What we showed on any date is replayable; corrections add to history, they don't erase it.
  5. Raw is one click away. Any rendered value can be traced to the cached payload and the upstream URL.

Not in this version

  • No generated narrative. The model-written daily summary was retired on 2026-09-24. Every sentence in the daily Brief is template-rendered directly from cached observations; no model writes any of it.
  • No replayable time machine. The snapshot history that powered day-by-day replay was destroyed on 2026-08-09 (see the disclosure above) and re-accumulation has not yet been re-enabled. The Brief archive is the record that accrues from 2026-09-24 onward.
  • No verification of upstream sources. This site relays instrument output with provenance attached. It does not certify that any upstream reading is true — it certifies where the reading came from and when.
  • Most of the original layers are retired. Each retired layer carries a dated, plain-English reason — the full ledger is on the status page. Retirement is a decision, not a failure, and is displayed as such.
  • No off-site archive copy yet. The Brief archive is committed daily to a local git repository on this server; an off-site mirror requires an owner-held deploy key and is flagged as pending until it exists.

Archive retention

Daily Briefs are retained indefinitely and byte-stable: the dated JSON is the citable record, its items are append-only, and corrections append with their own dates — they never overwrite (rule 4). Each day is committed to a git archive, so any after-the-fact edit to a past brief would be a visible diff, not a quiet fix.

Observation snapshots, when the history store resumes, follow this graduated retention rule, published here as the stated window: every snapshot for 7 days; the newest per 6 hours for 7–30 days; the newest per UTC day for 30 days–1 year; the newest per week for 1–5 years; the newest per month beyond that.
